# B27 — Slicer property tests (seeded random strips)

**Owner:** GLM builder · **Branch:** `B27` · **Date:** 2026-09-18

## Changes

- `tests/fixtures/strip_layouts.py` (new): `Segment` dataclass, `random_strip(seed, width=48, max_rows=6000)`
  and `art_strip(rows, width, seed)`. Layout decisions use `random.Random(seed)` (segment kinds, 65/35
  solid/gradient split, heights, noise amplitude, truncation); pixel noise and colours use
  `torch.Generator().manual_seed(seed)`. Segments strictly alternate art / non-art; the final segment is
  truncated to fit `max_rows` when at least 10 rows tall, otherwise dropped (so `H` ends up in
  `[max_rows-9, max_rows]`, always ≥ 200). Art rows are full-range noise; solid blocks are one random colour
  plus per-pixel noise with amp ∈ {0, 2}; gradient rows step from `c0` to `c1 = clamp(c0 + delta)`,
  `delta ∈ [-h//2, +h//2]`, one colour per row, no per-pixel noise.
- `tests/unit/test_slicer_properties.py` (new): a cached `run_case(seed)` (builds the strip and runs
  `row_stats` → `find_uniform_bands` → `plan_cuts` → `slice_strip` with `SMALL`) plus the twelve properties
  P1–P12 from the card. P1–P10 are parametrised over seeds 0–149 (one test function per property), P11 over
  40 seeds, P12 contains the two deterministic tall-strip tests (`DEFAULT` config).

## Findings

**None.** Every property passed for every seed on the first full run:

- P1: `find_uniform_bands` reproduced every solid/gradient segment ≥ 50 rows exactly (edges included) and
  never produced a band from shorter gutters, across all 150 seeds.
- P2–P7, P9, P10: band metadata, cut well-formedness, band-interior cuts, tiling, flags, the
  `hard_max_height` ceiling, the forced-cut precondition and determinism all held.
- P8: no oversized slice ever left an unused splittable centre — consistent with the DP being optimal.
- P11: `row_stats` is chunk-invariant for `chunk_rows ∈ {1, 7, 97, 8192}` on all 40 seeds.
- P12(a): the 16000-row pure-art strip yields exactly `ceil(16000/6000) - 1 = 2` forced cuts, 3 non-blank
  slices all ≤ `hard_max_height`. P12(b): the 18000-row strip (crossing the 8192-row chunk boundary) finds
  both gutters as bands, both gutter centres as cut candidates, every non-forced cut inside a gutter, and
  every slice ≤ `hard_max_height`.

Two non-findings worth recording for future cards:

- The only failure observed during development was in the test itself: I initially wrote the second P12(b)
  gutter as `[11000, 11060)` — arithmetic slip; 4000 + 200 + 7000 = 11200, so the gutter is `[11200, 11260)`.
  The strip construction matched the card all along. Fixed in the test, no slicer issue.
- Robustness margin on solid segments: with amp = 2 the per-row channel medians can wobble by ±1 (and
  pairwise by 2 in principle), but `find_uniform_bands` links rows when the *mean over channels* of the
  median step is `<= max_drift = 2.0`, and that mean is bounded by 2.0, so solid bands never split. Gradients
  step ≤ 1 per channel. This is why P1/P2 hold with headroom; a config with `max_drift < 1.0` could
  genuinely split amp-2 solid bands (not tested here — out of scope).

## Deviations

- `functools.cache` instead of the literal `functools.lru_cache` decoration the card sketched: `ruff`
  (UP033) requires `functools.cache` for `lru_cache(maxsize=None)`; behaviour is identical.
- The card's P12(b) row arithmetic implies the second gutter sits at `[11200, 11260)`, not `[11000, 11060)`;
  implemented per the arithmetic (see Findings).

## Questions

- None blocking. One observation for whoever tunes `SlicerConfig` defaults: the mean-over-channels drift
  test makes band linking quite tolerant of noisy solids (see the second non-finding above); if real raws
  produce noisy gutters, band splitting under the production `max_drift = 2.0` is theoretically possible only
  if a per-channel median step reaches 2 in *all three channels simultaneously* — unlikely but not
  impossible at very small widths.
